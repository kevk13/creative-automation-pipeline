30-second opening

"Enterprise marketing teams need hundreds of on-brand creative variants per campaign, and doing it by hand is too slow and too expensive. I built a six-step pipeline: a campaign brief in YAML or JSON goes in, and compliance-checked creatives come out. It loads and validates the brief, gathers or generates each product image, renders three aspect ratios, applies the brand overlay, organizes the outputs, then runs compliance. Every step has a typed Zod contract, every Claude call logs tokens and cost, and the whole thing is a deliberate two-to-three hour POC, so I scoped auth, queuing, and multi-tenancy out on purpose and documented them. Happy to go deep wherever you want." Then stop talking. Let them steer.

FDE talk-track reminders

  • Lead with the customer problem, not the tech. Every answer ties back to "what does the marketing team get."
  • Name your scope cuts before they do. "I deferred X on purpose" reads as senior. Getting caught reads as junior.
  • Make tradeoffs explicit and reversible: "I chose A over B because C, and here's when I'd revisit."
  • Show your observability: typed contracts, structured logs, a cost ledger. FDEs are trusted in prod, so prove you can see what's happening.
  • When you don't know, say so, then say how you'd find out. Confidence, not bluffing.

Ask them (pick 2-3) end strong

  • "When an FDE drops into a customer, how much is greenfield building vs. integrating into the customer's existing Adobe stack?"
  • "Where does the FDE handoff to the product or core engineering team usually happen, and what makes that handoff go badly?"
  • "What separates an FDE who's thriving here at one year from one who's struggling?"
  • "How much of the role is GenAI solutioning today vs. classic integration work, and where is that mix heading?"
  • "What's a customer problem your team is fighting right now that you wish was already solved?"
